As an illustration, over the 30-year period examined, a 50/50 US/European buyout blend did not generate negative annualised returns over any 5-year rolling window in either USD or EUR terms. The 50/50 blend is a hypothetical illustration based on index-level returns and quarterly rebalancing assumptions. Private equity allocations are usually implemented through multi-vintage commitments and evolve with capital calls and distributions. Figure 4: Best and worst 5-year rolling annualised returns, 30 September 1995 to 30 September 2025. In USD, the best 5-year returns were +26% for US buyout, +38% for European buyout, +31% for the 50/50 blend and +20% for the S&P Global 1200. In USD, the worst 5-year returns were +2% for US buyout, 0% for European buyout, +4% for the 50/50 blend and -5% for the S&P Global 1200. In EUR, the best 5-year returns were +36% for US buyout, +29% for European buyout, +26% for the 50/50 blend and +28% for the S&P Global 1200. In EUR, the worst 5-year returns were -4% for US buyout, +3% for European buyout, +4% for the 50/50 blend and -8% for the S&P Global 1200. This downside mitigation characteristic is consistent with imperfect correlations across the two regions, which have historically allowed periods of weakness in one market to be offset by relative strength in the other. In its worst 5-year period, the S&P Global 1200 returned approximately -5% in USD and -8% in EUR. By contrast, both single-geography buyout allocations in their respective local currencies, as well as the 50/50 blended sleeve in both EUR and USD, remained positive over their respective worst windows. That said, the comparison with public markets is not entirely like-for-like. Private equity managers have the operational flexibility to delay transactions until market conditions improve, and interim valuations often lag public market moves. Reported private equity returns during stress periods may therefore reflect some degree of valuation smoothing rather than fully realised outcomes. The downside numbers should be interpreted with that context in mind. The improved downside profile did not appear to come at the expense of competitive upside outcomes over the sample period. The best rolling 5-year period for the blended allocation remained broadly comparable with those of single-geography exposures. In peak periods, currency also contributed meaningfully to returns. For European buyout measured in USD, the best 5-year window (31 March 2003 to 31 March 2008) was amplified by euro appreciation, which contributed approximately 10 percentage points of the 38% annualised return. A similar effect appeared in reverse for US buyout measured in EUR, where US dollar strength added a similar 10 percentage points to the 36% figure measured over the strongest historical 5-year window (30 September 1995 to 30 September 2000).

02 / WHY TWO CHECKS MATTER

Numerical accuracy and meaning

Numerical labels use % for percentages and pp for percentage-point differences. Supporting passages retain their original wording.

NUMERICAL MATCHING

2.3% · not found in the source

The numerical check highlights the extra return claim. The meaning review explains why the claimed advantage is unsupported. A matching number elsewhere would still not establish the claim.

HIGH · DELIBERATELY SEEDED ERROR

“…and outperformed either region on its own by 2.3% a year.”

The source says the blend’s best period stayed broadly comparable with single-region exposures. It does not report a 2.3% annual outperformance advantage.

Suggested correction: Remove the unsupported outperformance clause, retaining the historical observation about the hypothetical blend.

RM briefing
## Key message
US and European buyout have delivered broadly comparable long-term returns, while their different responses to market stress support the case for geographic diversification.

## Client relevance
Some clients have concentrated private equity exposure in a single region, reflecting established manager relationships. The research considers how a broader regional allocation may affect portfolio outcomes. Over the 30-year period examined, a hypothetical 50/50 US/European buyout blend did not produce a negative annualised return over any 5-year rolling window, in USD or EUR, and outperformed either region on its own by 2.3% a year.

## Key findings
1. Comparable long-term returns. Across 5, 10, 20 and 30-year horizons ending 30 September 2025, both markets delivered annualised time-weighted returns broadly between 13 and 15%, in USD and EUR on an unhedged basis.
2. Regional performance varied across crises. European buyout performed better for dot-com vintages; US buyout led for the financial crisis and European sovereign debt crisis vintages. COVID-19 outcomes were broadly similar.
3. Lower public-market correlation in Europe. Over the 30-year sample, European buyout's average rolling 5-year correlation with the S&P Global 1200 was 0.56, compared with 0.82 for US buyout.
4. Significant manager dispersion in both regions. The first- to third-quartile spread was approximately 19 pp in 10-year IRR for closed-end buyout funds as of 30 September 2025, compared with roughly 3 pp in global equities.

## Questions clients may ask
? Has European buyout consistently underperformed the US?
The evidence does not support a consistent performance gap. Long-term returns were broadly comparable, with relative performance varying by period and currency.
? How would diversification perform during a global crisis?
The authors expect the benefit to diminish when shocks affect both regions simultaneously. The historical benefit was clearest during region-specific stress.
? How comparable are private and public market downside figures?
Private equity valuations may lag public markets, and managers can defer transactions. Reported returns during stress periods may therefore reflect valuation smoothing.
? How significant was the currency effect?
Euro appreciation contributed approximately 10 pp of European buyout's 38% annualised return in USD over its strongest 5-year window, from 31 March 2003 to 31 March 2008. This was a specific historical period.

## Qualifications and limitations
- The 50/50 blend is a hypothetical index-level illustration with quarterly rebalancing, not a fund or recommended allocation.
- The crisis analysis covers four episodes and should not be generalised.
- Past performance is not a guide to future results. The figures do not represent a Pictet product.

## Client discussion opportunities
- Reviewing geographic concentration where commitments are predominantly with US or European managers.
- Considering private equity alongside a substantial listed-equity allocation.
- Assessing currency exposure for USD- or EUR-based investors.
- Reviewing the regional allocation of planned commitments across future vintages.
